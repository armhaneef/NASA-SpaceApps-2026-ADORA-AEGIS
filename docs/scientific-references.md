# Scientific references

The astronaut physiology research in
[`astronaut-physiology-research.md`](./astronaut-physiology-research.md)
and the project guidance in the adjacent documents use the sources below.
They support scientific context and prototype design guidance only; they do
not establish clinical validity or operational suitability for AEGIS.

| Source | Date | Claims used | Review context |
|---|---|---|---|

[NTRS record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250000273) | 2025 | Altered-gravity cardiovascular deconditioning, headward/torso fluid redistribution, individual variability, and exercise as a countermeasure | NASA technical review from the Office of the Chief Health and Medical Officer; use as an overview, not as a source of personalized thresholds |
| Norsk, P., Asmar, A., Damgaard, M., & Christensen, N. | Mastrandrea, C. J., et al. *Lower body negative pressure identifies altered central vein characteristics without accompanying changes to baroreflexes in astronauts within hours of landing*. *Scientific Reports, 14*, 1215. | Rohr, J. J., et al. *Quantitative magnetic resonance image assessment of the optic nerve and surrounding sheath after spaceflight*. *npj Microgravity, 6*, 30. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-022-02492-z); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35469104/) | 2022 | Associations between selected brain structural measures and postflight motor/balance outcomes | Study of 14 astronauts; associations are not causal or validated individual predictions |
| Tu, D., et al. *Dynamic ensemble prediction of cognitive performance in spaceflight*. *Scientific Reports, 12*, 11032. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-14456-8); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35773291/) | 2022 | Fatigue, stress, prior PVT, and other covariates associated with repeated alertness measurements | Observational modeling study of 24 astronauts; outputs are not clinical indicators |
| Clark, T. K., et al. *Relationships Between Vestibular Measures as Potential Predictors for Spaceflight Sensorimotor Adaptation*. | Prisk, G. K. *The Lung in Space*. *Clinics in Chest Medicine, 26*(3), 415-438. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccm.2005.05.008) | 2005 | Pulmonary physiology and gravity-related changes in spaceflight | Review; not an individual clinical reference or AEGIS threshold source |
| Verbanck, S., Larsson, H., Linnarsson, D., Prisk, G. K., West, J. | Smith, S. M., Heer, M., Shackelford, L. C., Sibonga, J. D., Ploutz-Snyder, L., & Zwart, S. | Strock, N., et al. *Temporal Changes in Astronauts' Muscle and Cardiorespiratory Physiology Before, During, and After Spaceflight*. Use the source record and the underlying publication together where possible.
Do not treat catalog presence as clinical validation, unrestricted licensing,
or permission to redistribute subject-level data.

Do not treat synthetic examples, model outputs, or this prototype as evidence
of clinical validity or suitability for astronaut operations.
